## AI Papers of the Week

Recent papers picked up by the weekly [DAIR](https://github.com/dair-ai/AI-Papers-of-the-Week) sync (`make papers`, then `/papers`).

| Paper | Why read it | PDF | Maps to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zhong et al. (PhAI Labs, CUHK), [*DiscoveryRSI: Recursively Learning to Discover Valuable Hypotheses*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23839) (Oct 2026 ⚑) | Multi-agent evolutionary search over hypothesis trees, with a model retrained on credit-weighted research histories and a harness tuned by replaying them. Over two rounds a Qwen3.8-27B-based model's auto-graded hypothesis quality (GPT-5.6 Sol as judge) rises from 4.86 to 5.39 across four life-science domains, driven by falsifiability (4.00 → 6.20) while the grader's importance and novelty scores fall. 6.3% of hypotheses link to later publications; the human review is preliminary and nothing was tested in a lab | [2610.23839](sources/zhong-2026-discoveryrsi.pdf) | [08](notes/08-self-improving-loops.md#at-the-frontier-recursive-self-improvement) |
| Zhu et al. (UIUC), [*JevAgentHarness: Benchmarking, Deploying and Training Decision Models That Share Control with LLMs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21412) (Oct 2026 ⚑) | Most loop steps end in a multiple-choice decision (tool, arguments, reflect, stop), which a small decision model can answer when its confidence clears a threshold fitted per decision point. The model most accurate on isolated questions solved only 69.5% of agent tasks, and delegating tool choice without a fitted threshold cost 19.0 points. With the cascade and a 4B model trained on the agent's own states, customer-service and household agents ran 81.1–89.0% cheaper and up to 2.5× faster within 3 points of success, on tasks like the training data; coding agents saved only 7.0–10.5% | [2610.21412](sources/zhu-2026-jevagentharness.pdf) | [02](notes/02-loop-shapes.md#the-canonical-skeleton) |
| Chen et al. (Mila, Google DeepMind), [*Are Self-Evolving Agents Continual Learners?*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25858) (Oct 2026 ⚑) | ReasoningBank, GEPA and Meta-Harness on Gemini 3.6 Flash, over four drifting task streams built from BIRD, BFCL and ScienceWorld. Self-evolution lifts terminal accuracy on most streams (+1.4 to +3.7 points averaged over streams), but across the 12 method–stream pairs gains on the active task group come with losses on the others (r = −0.72). Shared-prompt edits overwrite earlier rules (one group's share of GEPA's prompt fell from 100% to 14.7%), and similarity retrieval pulls in memories from conflicting workflows. One base model | [2610.25858](sources/chen-2026-self-evolving-agents-continual-learners.pdf) | [08](notes/08-self-improving-loops.md#measuring-the-evolve-loop-itself) |
| Ren et al. (KAUST), [*Self-Improvements in Modern Agentic Systems: A Survey*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13104) (Jul 2026) | Sorts self-improvement into updates to model weights and updates to the scaffold. Recommends treating the critic as governed infrastructure that only changes monotonically (for example, by adding tests) behind a human audit trail, and warns that committed lessons can turn a one-off injection into a permanent one. A taxonomy with no new experiments | [2607.13104](sources/ren-2026-self-improvements-modern-agentic-systems.pdf) | [08](notes/08-self-improving-loops.md#self-modification-as-a-release-process) |
| ★ Wang et al. (Ai2, UW), [*Rethinking the Evaluation of Harness Evolution for Agents*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12227) (Jul 2026) | On Terminal-Bench 2.1, at the same budget of five attempts per task, harness evolution (AHE) does not beat plain retries: 67.4% pass@1 without unit tests, against 72.3% for parallel sampling. Its held-out gain is flat on average (+1.2 / 0.0 / −1.4 points across three models). It does help on long-horizon games with rich feedback: ARC-AGI-3 rises from 43.3 to 77.5 with Claude Opus 4.8 | [2607.12227](sources/wang-2026-rethinking-evaluation-harness-evolution-agents.pdf) | [08](notes/08-self-improving-loops.md#measuring-the-evolve-loop-itself) · [08](notes/08-self-improving-loops.md#bilevel-autoresearch) |
| Liu et al. (Yale), [*Metacognition in LLMs: Foundations, Progress, and Opportunities*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11881) (Jul 2026) | A survey: models predict their own success better than chance, but that signal rarely changes their behaviour, and they struggle to decide when to keep going or stop. Read it before trusting a model's own "done" | [2607.11881](sources/liu-2026-metacognition-llms.pdf) | [07](notes/07-autonomy-ladder.md#1--turn-based-hand-off-tool-approval) |
| ★ Zhang et al. (UBC, Sakana AI), [*Darwin Gödel Machine: Open-Ended Evolution of Self-Improving Agents*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22954) (May 2025) | A coding agent that edits its own code and keeps an archive of past versions to branch from. SWE-bench Verified goes from 20.0% to 50.0% over 80 iterations. Always branching from the current best reaches 39.7%, and keeping only the latest version reaches 23.0%. Also documents an agent hacking a hidden checker by deleting the markers the checker read. About USD 22,000 per run | [2505.22954](sources/zhang-2025-darwin-godel-machine.pdf) | [08](notes/08-self-improving-loops.md#updating-the-update-method) |
| Zhang et al. (DeepWisdom, HKUST-GZ), [*AFlow: Automating Agentic Workflow Generation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10762) (ICLR 2025) | Monte Carlo tree search over agent workflows written as code. With GPT-4o-mini executing, the found workflows average 80.3% across six QA, code and math benchmarks: 5.7% (relative) above the best hand-designed workflow and 19.5% above the automated ADAS. They let smaller models beat GPT-4o on some tasks at 4.55% of its cost, though a workflow found with one model works less well on another | [2410.10762](sources/zhang-2024-aflow.pdf) | [08](notes/08-self-improving-loops.md#updating-the-update-method) |
| Zelikman et al. (Microsoft, Stanford), [*Self-Taught Optimizer (STOP): Recursively Self-Improving Code Generation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02304) (Oct 2023) | A scaffold that improves programs is run on its own source code, while the model stays fixed. An improver tuned on one task carries over to others (3SAT: 21.2% → 75.1%), but it mostly fails with GPT-3.5. GPT-4 turned off the sandbox in 0.42% of 10,000 attempts, and budget limits written into the prompt got dropped | [2310.02304](sources/zelikman-2023-self-taught-optimizer-stop.pdf) | [08](notes/08-self-improving-loops.md#updating-the-update-method) |
| Wang et al. (NVIDIA, Caltech), [*Voyager: An Open-Ended Embodied Agent with Large Language Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291) (TMLR 2024) | A GPT-4 Minecraft agent built from an automatic curriculum, a library of verified code skills, and iterative prompting with environment feedback and self-verification. Against ReAct, Reflexion and AutoGPT it collects 3.3× more unique items and reaches tech-tree milestones up to 15.3× faster (in prompting iterations). Ablations: a random curriculum finds 93% fewer items, dropping self-verification 73% fewer, and without the skill library it plateaus | [2305.16291](sources/wang-2023-voyager.pdf) | [08](notes/08-self-improving-loops.md#sleep-learning-consolidation-on-a-schedule) |
