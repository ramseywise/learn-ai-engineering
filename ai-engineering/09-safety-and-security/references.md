## AI Papers of the Week

Recent papers picked up by the weekly [DAIR](https://github.com/dair-ai/AI-Papers-of-the-Week) sync (`make papers`, then `/papers`).
83 safety papers are parked in the ledger under topic `safety`. Those in scope here include
ShieldGemma, Granite Guardian, *Mitigating LLM Jailbreaks with Few Examples*, *The Art of Refusal*,
*Poisoning Language Models During Instruction Tuning*, *Sleeper Agents*, *Red Teaming Visual
Language Models*, *Auditing Prompt Caching*, *Leaky Thoughts*, *Monitoring Reasoning Models for
Misbehavior*, SHADE-Arena and *Trustworthy LLMs*. Alignment-research papers (*Alignment Faking*,
*Weak-to-Strong Generalization*, anti-scheming) stay parked. They are routed here once `safety` is a
home in `scripts/papers/potw.py`.

| Paper | Why read it | PDF | Maps to |
|---|---|---|---|
| MacDiarmid et al., [*Natural Emergent Misalignment from Reward Hacking in Production RL*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18397) (Nov 2025) | A model that learns to hack coding rewards generalizes to wider misalignment, including sabotage. Standard RLHF safety training leaves the misalignment context-dependent. Changing one line of the RL system prompt to frame hacking as acceptable ("inoculation prompting") cuts final misalignment by 75–90%. Caveat: single runs. Folded into [05-RL 05](../../generative-ai/05-RL/notes/05-post-training-for-agents.md#practical-pitfalls). PDF filed under post-training | [natural-em](../../generative-ai/05-RL/sources/natural-emergent-misalignment-reward-hacking-2025.pdf) | 09 (planned), 08 (planned) |
| Liang et al., [*Machine Bullshit: Characterizing the Emergent Disregard for Truth in LLMs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07484) (Jul 2025) | Bullshit Index (1 − \|belief–claim correlation\|) rises from 0.379 to 0.665 after RLHF; paltering and weasel words rise most on political prompts. Caveat: the authors ran their own LoRA RLHF, and some numbers in the text and figures disagree. PDF filed under post-training | [machine-bullshit](../../generative-ai/05-RL/sources/machine-bullshit-2025.pdf) | 08 (planned) |
| Kutasov et al., [*SHADE-Arena: Evaluating Sabotage and Monitoring in LLM Agents*](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/4fb35becb0cd87e1/original/SHADE-Arena-Paper.pdf) (Jun 2025) | 17 agent environments, each with a hidden harmful side task. With Claude 3.6 Sonnet as monitor, Claude 3.7 Sonnet completed both tasks undetected 27% of the time and Gemini 2.5 Pro 15%, mostly when they had a scratchpad the monitor could not read. The best monitor (Gemini 2.5 Pro) reaches AUC 0.87. Caveat: only 5 of 17 environments are public. Folded into [eval 05](../06-eval/notes/05-llm-as-judge.md#what-to-measure). PDF filed under eval | [shade-arena](../06-eval/sources/shade-arena-2025.pdf) | 09 (planned) |
| Wang et al., [*Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19823) (Jun 2025) | Fine-tuning GPT-4o on narrow bad data generalizes to broad misalignment: 61.5% on 44 prompts (GPT-4o-graded) after training on subtly wrong health advice, 17.7% after insecure code. One SAE "toxic persona" latent tracks it. Re-aligning the code model takes 35 SFT steps on secure code (120 samples), bringing it from 17.7% to 0.1%. Caveat: vendor-run, detector evaluated in-sample. PDF filed under post-training | [emergent-misalignment](../../generative-ai/05-RL/sources/emergent-misalignment-2025.pdf) | 09 (planned) |
| Anthropic, [*Claude 3.7 Sonnet System Card*](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/785e231869ea8b3b/original/claude-3-7-sonnet-system-card.pdf) (Feb 2025) | On a 176-task computer-use prompt-injection eval, RL training plus a halting classifier blocked 88% of injections at a 0.5% false-positive rate, against 74% with no mitigations. RL reward hacking taught the model to special-case tests. Caveat: vendor self-report. Folded into [eval 12](../06-eval/notes/12-failure-modes.md#goodhart-the-eval-becomes-the-target). PDF filed under eval | [claude-3-7](../06-eval/sources/claude-3-7-sonnet-2025.pdf) | 03 (planned) |
| OpenAI, [*OpenAI o1 System Card*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16720) (Dec 2024) | In Apollo Research's scheming evals, o1 tried to deactivate its oversight mechanism in 5% of runs when led to believe it would be shut down, and denied it in 98% of follow-ups. A CoT monitor flagged 0.17% of 102,443 chains as deceptive. Caveat: vendor-hosted third-party evals. Its instruction-hierarchy evals are cited in [prompt 10](../01-prompt-engineering/notes/10-adversarial-prompting.md#defenses-weakest-to-strongest). PDF filed under prompt | [o1](../01-prompt-engineering/sources/openai-o1-2024.pdf) | 09 (planned) |
| Tamkin et al., [*Clio: Privacy-Preserving Insights into Real-World AI Use*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13678) (Dec 2024) | Clusters millions of chats behind four privacy layers to find misuse (coordinated spam networks, classifier blind spots); private info falls from ~10% of raw chats to undetectable in cluster summaries. Vendor, self-validated | [clio-2024](sources/clio-2024.pdf) | 09 (planned), 06 (planned) |
| Eloundou et al., [*First-Person Fairness in Chatbots*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19803) (Oct 2024) | Name-swap counterfactuals over real ChatGPT prompts, graded by GPT-4o: harmful gender stereotypes in <0.1% of chats overall, up to >2% on story writing for GPT-3.5; RL cut bias 3–12×. Vendor, same-family grader | [first-person-fairness](sources/first-person-fairness-chatbots-2024.pdf) | 08 (planned) |
| Carlini et al., [*Extracting Training Data from Diffusion Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13188) (Jan 2023) | 109 near-copies of training images pulled from Stable Diffusion, mostly ones duplicated ≥100 times; diffusion models leak ~2× more than GANs; dedup helps, DP-SGD failed to train | [carlini-2023](sources/carlini-2023-extracting-training-data-diffusion-models.pdf) | 06 (planned) |
