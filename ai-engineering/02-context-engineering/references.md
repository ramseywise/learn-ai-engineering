Recent papers picked up by the weekly [DAIR](https://github.com/dair-ai/AI-Papers-of-the-Week) sync (`make papers`, then `/papers`).

| Paper | Why read it | PDF | Maps to |

| ★ Peng, [*MemoryTax: The Bitter Lesson of Agent Memory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29823) (Oct 2026) | Mem0 beats full context only with GPT-4o-mini; with stronger models designed memory ties or loses and its build costs more than a full-context run. A coding agent searching the raw history scores 99% at under half the cost. The gain holds even at 256K, where nothing overflows | [2610.02163](sources/zhang-2026-autocompact.pdf) | [05](notes/05-compression-compaction.md#trigger-points) |

Mid-context edits break prefix caching, and editable context gives injections a place to persist | [2609.37725](sources/shao-2026-context-language-models.pdf) | [05](notes/05-compression-compaction.md#letting-the-model-edit-its-own-context) |
| ★ Zerhoudi, Mitrović & Granitzer (Passau), [*The Compaction Cliff in Long-Running AI Agent Memory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22752) (Aug 2026) | Claude Code's /compact on Sonnet 4.6 keeps 53% of safety rules after one round and 10% after five, across 20 agent configurations. Type-aware compaction pins rules verbatim and holds 0.96. Without the deterministic verifier, a reported 1.00 hid 57% dropped constraints | [2608.22752](sources/zerhoudi-2026-compaction-cliff-long-running-ai.pdf) | [05](notes/05-compression-compaction.md#retention-priority) |

## About the upstream course
