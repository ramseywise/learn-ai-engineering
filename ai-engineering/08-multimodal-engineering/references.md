| Paper | Why read it | PDF | Maps to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kimi Team, [Kimi K2.5: Visual Agentic Intelligence](https://github.com/MoonshotAI/Kimi-K2.5/blob/master/tech_report.pdf) (2026) | Vision added to the 1.04T-total / 32B-active K2 with ~15T vision-text tokens. At a fixed token budget, early fusion with a 10% vision mix beats late fusion with 50% (vision reasoning 43.8 vs 39.0, OCR 65.7 vs 61.5). SFT on text only is enough to unlock vision tool use, and vision RL lifts text scores (MMLU-Pro 84.7 → 86.4). Agent Swarm raises BrowseComp from 60.6 to 78.4. Caveats: many baseline scores are the vendor's own re-runs, and Swarm Bench is in-house. No arXiv stamp on the PDF | [kimi-k2-5](sources/kimi-k2-5-visual-agentic-2026.pdf) | 02 (planned), 01 (planned) |
| Kimi Team, [Kimi-Audio Technical Report](https://github.com/MoonshotAI/Kimi-Audio/blob/master/assets/kimia_report.pdf) (2025) | Audio LLM on Qwen2.5-7B with 12.5 Hz semantic tokens plus Whisper features as input, separate text and audio heads, and a chunked flow-matching detokenizer. Trained on 13M hours of audio. LibriSpeech WER 1.28/2.42, VoiceBench 76.93 vs 72.83 for Qwen2.5-Omni. Production serving is turn-based behind voice activity detection, not full duplex. Caveats: vendor-built eval toolkit, GPT-4o-mini as judge, no latency numbers | [kimi-audio](sources/kimi-audio-2025.pdf) | 05 (planned) |
| Qwen Team, [Qwen2.5-Omni Technical Report](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen2.5-Omni/blob/main/assets/Qwen2.5_Omni.pdf) (2025) | One 7B model that takes text, image, audio and video and streams speech back, split into a Thinker (the LLM) and a Talker that reads its hidden states. TMRoPE gives one time ID per 40 ms so audio and video interleave. The cost of omni: MMLU-Pro 47.0 vs 56.3 for Qwen2.5-7B. Speech-instruction MMLU 65.6 vs 69.3 for text input. Caveat: it discusses first-packet latency but never measures it | [qwen2-5-omni](sources/qwen2-5-omni-2025.pdf) | 05 (planned) |
| ★ Chen et al., [Janus-Pro: Unified Multimodal Understanding and Generation with Data and Model Scaling](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/Janus/blob/main/janus_pro_tech_report.pdf) (2025) | Janus scaled to 7B with 72M synthetic aesthetic images (1:1 with real). GenEval 0.80 vs 0.67 for DALL-E 3 and 0.74 for SD3-Medium; DPG-Bench 84.19 vs 83.50. Output is still 384×384, and the report concedes weak fine detail and small faces, so GenEval here measures prompt compliance, not image quality. Self-reported evals | [janus-pro](sources/janus-pro-2025.pdf) | [06](notes/06-generating-images-and-video.md#how-a-diffusion-model-makes-an-image) |
| Wu et al., [DeepSeek-VL2: Mixture-of-Experts Vision-Language Models for Advanced Multimodal Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10302) (2024) | MoE VLMs with 1.0/2.8/4.5B active parameters. Dynamic tiling splits an image into up to 9 SigLIP 384 px tiles plus a thumbnail, and pixel shuffle cuts each tile from 729 to 196 tokens: about 1,023 visual tokens for a 2×2 grid and 2,017 for 3×3. Tiling turns off above 2 images. DocVQA 93.3, OCRBench 811. Fits on one GPU of 10/40/80 GB | [2412.10302](sources/wu-2024-deepseek-vl2.pdf) | 02 (planned) |
| Wu et al., [Janus: Decoupling Visual Encoding for Unified Multimodal Understanding and Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13848) (2024) | One autoregressive transformer that reads with SigLIP and draws with VQ tokens. The ablation is the reason to read it: sharing the VQ tokenizer for understanding drops POPE from 87.0 to 60.1 and MMBench from 69.4 to 35.0 at 1.3B. Classifier-free guidance applies to logits (scale 5). GenEval 0.61 vs 0.55 for SDXL | [2410.13848](sources/wu-2024-janus.pdf) | [06](notes/06-generating-images-and-video.md#how-a-diffusion-model-makes-an-image) |
| Polyak et al., [Movie Gen: A Cast of Media Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13720) (2024) | A 30B flow-matching transformer on video latents compressed 8×8×8, which puts a 16 s 768 px clip at 16 fps in a ~73K-token context. Meta's own human eval gives net win rates on overall quality of +35.0 against Runway Gen3, +8.2 against Sora and +3.9 against Kling 1.5 (on par). Caveat: Sora could only be judged on OpenAI's published videos, against a hand-picked best of 5 from Movie Gen. PDF filed under llm-fundamentals | [movie-gen](../../generative-ai/01-llm-fundamentals/sources/movie-gen-2024.pdf) | [06](notes/06-generating-images-and-video.md#video) |
| Défossez et al., [Moshi: a speech-text foundation model for real-time dialogue](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00037) (2024) | Full-duplex speech: user and model audio are separate streams, so there are no turns. Mimi codec at 12.5 Hz and 1.1 kbps. Latency is 160 ms in theory and 200 ms in practice. Predicting the text before the audio (Inner Monologue) roughly triples spoken QA accuracy, and audio training costs text knowledge: MMLU falls from 54.3 for the base Helium LM to 49.7 for Moshi. Also the clearest evidence that codec re-encoding erases audio watermarks | [moshi](sources/moshi-2024.pdf) | 05 (planned), [06](notes/06-generating-images-and-video.md#provenance-and-training-data-risk) |
| Zhou et al., [Transfusion: Predict the Next Token and Diffuse Images with One Multi-Modal Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11039) (2024) | One transformer with next-token loss on text and diffusion loss on continuous image patches. On its scaling curves it matches a Chameleon-style discrete-token model's image FID with 34× less compute. The 7.3B model scores GenEval 0.63 vs 0.55 for SDXL and 0.68 for SD3, which used synthetic captions. PDF filed under llm-fundamentals | [2408.11039](../../generative-ai/01-llm-fundamentals/sources/zhou-2024-transfusion.pdf) | [06](notes/06-generating-images-and-video.md#how-a-diffusion-model-makes-an-image), 01 (planned) |
| Chameleon Team, [Chameleon: Mixed-Modal Early-Fusion Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09818) (2024) | Early fusion: a 512×512 image becomes 1,024 tokens from an 8,192-entry codebook, trained alongside text on ~10T tokens. Training diverged without QK-norm and z-loss (7B also needed dropout). The tokenizer is weak on text-heavy images. Human-eval wins over Gemini+ (60.4%) and GPT-4V+ (51.6%) come from a vendor-run eval with inter-rater α = 0.338 | [2405.09818](sources/team-2024-chameleon.pdf) | [06](notes/06-generating-images-and-video.md#how-a-diffusion-model-makes-an-image), 01 (planned) |
| Bai et al., [Hallucination of Multimodal Large Language Models: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18930) (2024) | Taxonomy (object category, attribute, relation), causes (no negative examples in training, so models default to "yes"; the LLM prior overriding vision) and a map of benchmarks: CHAIR, POPE, AMBER, HallusionBench, MMHal. Mitigations from higher input resolution to contrastive decoding and post-hoc correction (Woodpecker). The authors concede POPE's yes/no format doesn't match real use and that most fixes are empirical | [2404.18930](sources/bai-2024-hallucination-multimodal-large-language-models.pdf) | 07 (planned) |
| Zhang et al., [MM-LLMs: Recent Advances in MultiModal Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13601) (2024) | Most multimodal LLMs share five parts: modality encoder, input projector, LLM, output projector and generator. Training usually updates only ~2% of parameters, mostly the projectors. Recipes: higher input resolution, high-quality SFT data, and mixing text-only instruction data back in to protect text skills. Caveat: its benchmark tables are copied from the papers, not re-run. PDF filed under llm-fundamentals | [2401.13601](../../generative-ai/01-llm-fundamentals/sources/zhang-2024-mm-llms.pdf) | 01 (planned), 02 (planned) |
| Sun et al., [Aligning Large Multimodal Models with Factually Augmented RLHF (LLaVA-RLHF)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14525) (2023) | First RLHF'd VLM, plus MMHal-Bench, a hallucination-penalizing benchmark. With a 7B policy, standard RLHF lifts LLaVA-Bench from 86.3 (SFT) to 93.4 while MMHal-Bench stays at 1.8: PPO games the reward model with longer answers. Giving the reward model the image captions (Fact-RLHF) reaches 94.1 and 2.1. Folded into post-training as a reward-hacking example | [llava-rlhf](../../generative-ai/05-RL/sources/sun-2023-aligning-large-multimodal-models-factually.pdf) | 07 (planned) |
| Yang et al., [The Dawn of LMMs: Preliminary Explorations with GPT-4V(ision)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17421) (2023) | A 166-page catalogue of hand-picked GPT-4V examples, deliberately without benchmarks. Useful for visual referring prompting (drawing arrows and boxes on the input image) and for using a VLM to judge or rewrite text-to-image prompts. Weak on reliability: the authors say some cases work only with tailored prompts | [2309.17421](sources/yang-2023-dawn-lmms.pdf) | [06](notes/06-generating-images-and-video.md#sweep-dont-guess), 02 (planned) |
| Bai et al., [Qwen-VL: A Versatile Vision-Language Model for Understanding, Localization, Text Reading, and Beyond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12966) (2023) | A 9.6B open VLM trained in 3 stages. It reaches 448 px input and 256 compressed visual tokens, and emits bounding boxes as plain text. DocVQA 65.1, TextVQA 63.8, RefCOCO val 89.4. Mixing pure-text data into training kept MMLU at 50.7 vs 49.9 for its LLM init. Caveat: VQAv2, GQA, DocVQA and TextVQA are in its stage-2 training mix, so those scores are not zero-shot | [2308.12966](sources/bai-2023-qwen-vl.pdf) | 02 (planned), 03 (planned) |
| Awadalla et al., [OpenFlamingo: An Open-Source Framework for Training Large Autoregressive Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01390) (2023) | An open replication of Flamingo (3B–9B) that reaches 80–89% of Flamingo's scores. Its useful negative results: on VQAv2, OF-9B scores 30.5% on counting questions vs 70.6% on yes/no. Retrieving similar in-context examples adds +19.8 AUC on HatefulMemes (32-shot) but costs −20.3 CIDEr on Flickr30K (0-shot), where the model parrots neighbours' captions | [2308.01390](sources/awadalla-2023-openflamingo.pdf) | 02 (planned) |
| Guo et al., [AnimateDiff: Animate Your Personalized Text-to-Image Diffusion Models without Specific Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04725) (2023) | A motion module trained once on SD 1.5 plugs into any personalized SD 1.5 checkpoint. A MotionLoRA (~30 MB) learns a camera move from 20–50 clips in 1–2 hours. Caveat: the evidence is a user ranking and CLIP scores against two adapted baselines, on prompts the authors chose. PDF filed under llm-fundamentals | [2307.04725](../../generative-ai/01-llm-fundamentals/sources/guo-2023-animatediff.pdf) | [06](notes/06-generating-images-and-video.md#video) |
| Dai et al., [InstructBLIP: Towards General-purpose Vision-Language Models with Instruction Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06500) (2023) | Instruction tuning BLIP-2's Q-Former on 13 datasets, with 13 held out. The clean result: on unseen datasets, instruction templates beat plain multitask training (held-out avg 52.9 vs 45.5–46.8, FlanT5-XL), while the two tie on seen ones | [2305.06500](sources/dai-2023-instructblip.pdf) | 02 (planned) |
| Blattmann et al., [Align your Latents: High-Resolution Video Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08818) (2023) | Temporal layers inserted between a frozen Stable Diffusion's spatial layers, plus a video-tuned decoder: reconstruction FVD on driving footage falls from 390.88 to 32.94. The temporal layers transfer to DreamBooth checkpoints without retraining. Zero-shot UCF-101 FVD 550.61 vs 367.23 for Make-A-Video. PDF filed under llm-fundamentals | [2304.08818](../../generative-ai/01-llm-fundamentals/sources/blattmann-2023-align-your-latents.pdf) | [06](notes/06-generating-images-and-video.md#video) |
| Liu et al., [Visual Instruction Tuning (LLaVA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485) (2023) | The recipe most open VLMs copied: CLIP ViT-L/14, a linear projector and Vicuna, tuned on 158K GPT-4-generated instructions. It is evaluated by GPT-4 as judge on small sets (30 COCO images; 24 in-the-wild images). Its own failure analysis is the useful part: the model says "yes" to strawberry yogurt when the fridge holds only yogurt and strawberries ("bag of patches") | [2304.08485](sources/liu-2023-visual-instruction-tuning.pdf) | 02 (planned), 07 (planned) |
| Oquab et al., [DINOv2: Learning Robust Visual Features without Supervision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07193) (2023) | Self-supervised image features that match OpenCLIP on ImageNet linear probe (86.5 vs 86.2) and beat it by a wide margin on retrieval and dense tasks (Oxford-Hard 54.0 vs 19.7). The case for choosing a non-CLIP encoder when pixels matter more than captions | [2304.07193](sources/oquab-2023-dinov2.pdf) | 01 (planned) |
| Kang et al., [Scaling up GANs for Text-to-Image Synthesis (GigaGAN)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05511) (2023) | A 1B-parameter GAN that makes a 512 px image in 0.13 s. Zero-shot COCO FID 9.09 vs 9.62 for SD v1.5 (authors' runs). Scaling a plain StyleGAN2 5.7× made FID worse (29.91 → 34.07); attention and architecture changes were what made it scale. Its upsampler scores FID 1.54 vs 9.39 for the SD upscaler on 64→256 ImageNet. The authors concede realism trails DALL·E 2. PDF filed under llm-fundamentals | [2303.05511](../../generative-ai/01-llm-fundamentals/sources/kang-2023-scaling-up-gans-text-image.pdf) | [06](notes/06-generating-images-and-video.md#how-a-diffusion-model-makes-an-image) |
| Wu et al., [Visual ChatGPT: Talking, Drawing and Editing with Visual Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04671) (2023) | ChatGPT orchestrating 22 vision tools through a prompt manager. Worth reading for the tool-description format and the lineage-encoding filenames. Case studies only, no quantitative eval | [2303.04671](sources/wu-2023-visual-chatgpt.pdf) | [06](notes/06-generating-images-and-video.md#editing-img2img-inpainting-control-images) |

| Huang et al., [Language Is Not All You Need: Aligning Perception with Language Models (Kosmos-1)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045) (2023) | A 1.6B MLLM trained from scratch on interleaved web data.
