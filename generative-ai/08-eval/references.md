## Papers

### Judges and graders

| | Paper | arXiv | Chapters |
|---|---|---|---|
| ★ | Zheng et al., *Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena* (NeurIPS 2023 Datasets & Benchmarks): GPT-4 matched human experts' pairwise preferences about 85% of the time (ties excluded), about the human–human rate. Position bias was the solid finding: under answer-swapping GPT-4 kept its verdict 65% of the time, Claude-v1 23.8%. Verbosity bias was also shown; the authors call their self-preference data too limited to establish it (GPT-3.5 did not favour itself). [PDF](../05-RL/sources/zheng-2023-judging-llm-as-judge-mt.pdf) | [2306.05685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685) | [05](notes/05-llm-as-judge.md#known-biases) · [15](notes/15-calibration-against-human-labels.md#the-procedure) |
| | Liu et al., *G-Eval: NLG Evaluation using GPT-4 with Better Human Alignment* (2023): judge-generated evaluation steps | [2303.16634](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16634) | 05 |
| ★ | Shankar et al., *Who Validates the Validators? Aligning LLM-Assisted Evaluation of LLM Outputs with Human Preferences* (UIST 2024): EvalGen picks assertions from a user's grades, and criteria drift as people grade. Small study: two pipelines offline, nine practitioners. [PDF](sources/shankar-2024-who-validates-the-validators.pdf) | [2404.12272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12272) | 02, 12, 14, 15 |
| | Shankar et al., *SPADE: Synthesizing Data Quality Assertions for Large Language Model Pipelines* (2024): candidate assertions from prompt changes, some of which call an LLM, filtered on labelled examples. | ★ | Marioriyad et al., *The Judge Who Never Admits: Hidden Shortcuts in LLM-based Evaluation* (2026, preprint): pairwise judges shift on metadata cues, often without saying so, though acknowledgement reaches 76% in some cells. [PDF](sources/marioriyad-2026-judge-who-never-admits.pdf) | [2602.07996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07996) | 14 |
| ★ | Tapwal et al., *Faithful or Fabricated? A Causal Framework for Rationalization Bias in LLM Judges* (2026, preprint): locking quoted evidence before the verdict cuts revision after a label reveal, at the cost of ties on 82 to 94% of near-equivalent pairs; reasoning first without locking was the most cue-prone. | | Mittal & Arike, *C2-Faith: Benchmarking LLM Judges for Causal and Coverage Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning* (2026, preprint): on maths chains, judges detect an error far better than they locate it, flag 40 to 55% of perfect chains and overrate completeness. [PDF](sources/mittal-2026-c2-faith.pdf) | [2603.05167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05167) | 14 |
| ★ | Turpin et al., *Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think: Unfaithful Explanations in Chain-of-Thought Prompting* (NeurIPS 2023): 1 of 426 explanations supporting a biased prediction mentions the bias. [PDF](../../ai-engineering/01-prompt-engineering/sources/turpin-2023-language-models-don-t-always.pdf) | [2305.04388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04388) | 14 |
| | Lanham et al., *Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning* (2023): edits the generated reasoning to see whether the answer depends on it, and the dependence varies widely by task. [PDF](sources/lanham-2023-measuring-faithfulness-cot.pdf) | [2307.13702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13702) | 14 |
| | Bentham, Stringham & Marasović, *Chain-of-Thought Unfaithfulness as Disguised Accuracy* (TMLR 2024): Lanham's with-and-without-reasoning metric, once normalised, tracks accuracy (R² = 0.74). [PDF](sources/bentham-2024-cot-unfaithfulness-disguised-accuracy.pdf) | [2402.14897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14897) | 14 |
| | Chen et al., *Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think* (Anthropic, 2025): Claude 3.7 Sonnet and DeepSeek R1 reveal hint use 25% and 39% of the time overall. Answering models, not judges. [PDF](sources/chen-2025-reasoning-models-dont-always-say.pdf) | [2505.05410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05410) | 14 |
| | Ratner et al., *Snorkel: Rapid Training Data Creation with Weak Supervision* (PVLDB 2017): correlated labelling functions mislead a model that assumes independence; the idea behind note 14's independence limit. [PDF](sources/ratner-2017-snorkel.pdf) | [1711.10160](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10160) | 14 |
| | Guo et al., *On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks* (2017): confidence calibration background; [DS 01/04](../../data-science/01-classical-ml/notes/04-model-evaluation.md#calibration-is-a-08-really-80) covers calibration on a classifier.
